Behind the Genes: Why BRCA Testing is a Must for Women’s Health

May 1, 2024
5 min read

In the realm of women’s health, knowledge is a powerful tool. Understanding your genetic makeup can provide invaluable insights into your risk for certain conditions, especially breast and ovarian cancers.

Empowerment Through Information

BRCA1 and BRCA2 are human genes that produce tumor suppressor proteins. When either of these genes is mutated, or altered, DNA damage may not be repaired properly. As a result, cells are more likely to develop additional genetic alterations that can lead to cancer.

Early detection through BRCA testing allows for proactive management, regular screenings, and informed lifestyle choices that can significantly reduce risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumor suppressor genes. Mutations in these genes significantly increase a person's risk of developing breast and ovarian cancers.

Testing is recommended for individuals with a strong family history of breast, ovarian, tubal, or peritoneal cancer, or if a relative is a known BRCA mutation carrier.

A positive result means an increased risk, not a cancer diagnosis. It allows you to explore proactive options like enhanced screening, lifestyle changes, or preventive surgeries.
