What is NIPS and How It Is Transforming Prenatal Care

Non-invasive Prenatal Screening (NIPS) analyses cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) circulating in maternal blood to assess fetal chromosomal status without invasive sampling. During pregnancy, trophoblastic cells from the placenta release DNA fragments into the maternal bloodstream; NIPS selectively evaluates these fragments to screen for common aneuploidies. Because the test requires only a maternal blood draw, it avoids the traditional procedure related miscarriage risk. This safety advantage has changed prenatal care by enabling earlier, broadly accessible screening.

NIPS demonstrates high sensitivity and specificity for major trisomies: reported detection rates approximate 99% for trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), 97-98% for trisomy 18 (Edward syndrome), and about 95% for trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome). Leading professional societies including ACOG, SMFM, ACMG, and the International Society for Prenatal Diagnosis endorse NIPS as the most effective screening modality currently available. While NIPS substantially improves risk assessment, it remains a screening test; positive or diagnostic uncertainty should prompt confirmatory diagnostic testing and genetic counseling to guide clinical decision-making.

What NIPS Screens For

Understanding what NIPS can and cannot detect helps inform your decision.

01
Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21)

Detection: 99%
Most common chromosomal condition. Intellectual disability range from mild to moderate. Early detection enables informed preparation and family support.

02
Edward Syndrome (Trisomy 18)

Detection: 97-98%
Severe condition with multiple birth defects. Difficult prognosis. Early detection enables informed decision-making about pregnancy continuation.

03
Patau Syndrome (Trisomy 13)

Detection: 95%
Rare but serious condition. Severe skeletal deformities and major organ defects. Early detection allows pregnancy planning.

04
Expanded Screening (Optional)

Expanded Screening Includes
In addition to basic NIPS, it also includes detection of specific microdelections including 22q11 deletion, 1p36 deletions, and others.

05
What NIPS Doesn't Screen For

Neural tube defects (spina bifida), heart defects, cleft palate, most gene mutations. NIPS screens for common chromosomal abnormalities, not all possible birth defects.

How NIPS Works: Five Simple Steps

From blood draw to results, NIPS is a straightforward, transparent process.

1
Sample Collection
A standard blood draw is performed at any authorized collection center, similar to routine blood testing. The sample is collected in specialized NIPS tubes with none to minimal discomfort.
2
Sample Processing
The sample is securely transported to the laboratory, where cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) is extracted. Quality control measures ensure adequate fetal fraction for reliable analysis.
3
DNA Sequencing & Analysis
The extracted DNA undergoes advanced sequencing, followed by bioinformatic analysis to evaluate chromosome representation and identify potential chromosomal abnormalities.
4
Expert Interpretation
Certified genetic professionals analyze the sequencing data and calculate the likelihood of chromosomal conditions, providing comprehensive clinical interpretation.
5
Results & Genetic Counseling
Results are delivered within 7–14 working days of sample collection, along with a comprehensive report and genetic counseling support to help you understand the findings.
